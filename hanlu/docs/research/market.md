### 1.2 Takeaways and gaps

1. **No serious French-first, HSK 3.0-native product exists.** French learners either use EN apps (Pleco, Skritter, Du Chinese, Hack Chinese are English-only) or FR apps built on outdated 2.0 lists. **Gap #1**
2. **Confusion over the two sets of numbers** (GF0025 vs the 2025 exam syllabus) is widespread. Whoever explains it clearly wins search traffic and trust. **Gap #2**
3. **Speaking is now mandatory from HSK 3**, and translation is added at 5–6. Few apps offer HSKK-3.0-style drills, and none offer FR↔ZH translation practice. **Gap #3**
4. Exam-prep tools (mock tests in the new format, timed sections) sit mostly on Chinese-market sites (hskmock etc.) and are rarely localised.
5. The web beats mobile-only for SEO. Most competitors are app-first and gain little from organic search, except Hack Chinese, Mandarin Zone and StudyCLI, which rank on "HSK 3.0" queries.

## 2. Positioning recommendation

**One-liner (FR):** « Préparez le nouveau HSK 3.0, en français : listes officielles, révisions intelligentes, oral et examens blancs. »
**One-liner (EN):** "The HSK 3.0 exam, made clear: official lists, smart review, speaking drills and mock exams."

- **Primary ICP:** French-speaking adult learners (France, Belgium, Switzerland, Québec, francophone Africa) preparing for an HSK exam for university, job or visa reasons. **Secondary:** English speakers who want clean HSK 3.0 prep.
- **Wedge:** the most accurate free HSK 3.0 reference on the web (levels, lists, grammar) → a free SRS account → a paid exam-prep pass.
- **Differentiators:** (1) bilingual FR/EN glosses on every word and grammar point; (2) the exam syllabus as the source of truth, with GF0025 shown for context; (3) speaking and handwriting drills limited to the official lists; (4) mock tests in the 3.0 format once CTI publishes samples.
- **Avoid:** competing head-on with HelloChinese or Duolingo on gamification, or with Pleco on the dictionary.

## 3. Freemium and paywall design

### 3.1 Free vs paid

| Free (SEO + activation) | Paid "Hanlu Pro" |
|---|---|
| All public word lists and grammar pages (indexable) | Full SRS across all levels (free = HSK 1 + first 100 words of HSK 2, or 20 new cards/day) |
| HSK 1 course complete | HSK 2–6 courses, then 7–9 |
| The "HSK 3.0 explained" page, level quiz / placement test | Mock exams in 3.0 format with timed sections and a score estimate |
| 1 sample mock section per level | Speaking (HSKK) practice with pronunciation feedback |
| Handwriting for HSK 2 list (first 50 characters) | Full handwriting lists, offline/PWA, progress analytics, FR↔ZH translation drills |

**Paywall moments (highest intent):** finishing HSK 1, starting a mock exam, hitting the daily new-card cap, and the "exam date countdown" set during onboarding.

### 3.2 Price points (EUR, VAT included)

| Plan | Price | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | **€9.99** | In line with HelloChinese / Hack Chinese / Skritter ($12–15) |
| **Annual (default, highlighted)** | **€59.99** (≈ €5/mo, "−50%") | Below Du Chinese / ChineseSkill ($80) and Hack Chinese ($96) |
| Exam pass, 3 months, one-off | **€24.99** | Matches exam cycles (13 Dec 2026 launch, then regular sessions); buyers who dislike subscriptions |
| Lifetime | **€149** (founder offer, capped, e.g. first 500) | Early cash plus word of mouth. Keep it capped: lifetime revenue hurts MRR and valuation multiples |
| Student / education | −30% with student ID; later a classroom plan at €3–4 per seat per month | Instituts Confucius, lycées with Chinese LV2/LV3 |

- Offer a 7-day free trial on the annual plan only, and aim for more than 60% of paid volume on annual.
- Billing: Stripe on the web (lower fees than 30% app-store cut); if apps come later, use RevenueCat.
- Show VAT-inclusive EUR; handle EU VAT OSS (Stripe Tax).

## 5. Metrics a buyer would diligence, and the events to capture

### 5.1 What buyers look at (content/SaaS marketplaces: Acquire.com, Empire Flippers, FE International)

| Metric | Definition | Healthy target (consumer edtech, rough) |
|---|---|---|
| **MRR / ARR** | Normalised recurring revenue (annual ÷ 12); lifetime and passes reported separately | Growing ≥ 5% MoM early |
| **Net revenue / profit (SDE)** | Trailing 12 months, with add-backs | Valuations ≈ 3–5× annual profit for small SaaS/content **[estimate]** |
| **Logo churn / revenue churn** | Monthly cancellations ÷ start-of-month payers | Monthly plans 8–12%; annual renewal ≥ 35–50% |
| **CAC & payback** | Blended paid spend ÷ new payers; organic share | Payback < 6 months; organic > 60% of signups |
| **Activation rate** | % of signups who finish their first lesson and 20 reviews within 24 h | ≥ 40% |
| **Free→paid conversion** | Payers ÷ signups within 30/90 days | 2–5% (freemium), 25–40% trial→paid |
| **Retention cohorts** | D1/D7/D30 active; M1–M12 paid retention by signup month | D30 ≥ 15% |
| **Traffic sources** | GSC clicks, organic share, top pages, branded vs non-branded | Diversified; no single page > 30% |
| **LTV** | ARPU × gross margin ÷ churn | LTV:CAC ≥ 3 |
| **Refunds / chargebacks, email list size, SEO asset** | Stripe and ESP exports; domain authority, indexed pages | |

Keep data portable: Stripe as the billing source of truth, a monthly metrics CSV, and a GSC export. Buyers want 12–24 months of verifiable history.

Identity: anonymous ID linked to user_id at signup. Record events **server-side** where possible: Stripe webhooks for revenue, API handlers for learning events. This makes them robust to ad-blockers and keeps them out of the device-storage consent scope.

### 5.3 Recommended analytics stack
- **Audience/traffic (no banner):** **Plausible** (EU-hosted, cookieless, from €9/mo) or **Matomo** in the CNIL-exempt configuration. Both let the site run without a consent banner for audience measurement.
- **Product analytics (activation, cohorts):** **PostHog EU Cloud** (Frankfurt). Use it server-side for logged-in events, or in the browser only after consent. Cookieless mode is possible, but the CNIL treats product analytics and session replay as needing consent, so gate the client SDK on consent.
- **Revenue:** Stripe (plus Stripe Sigma, or ChartMogul's free tier) for MRR, churn and LTV.
- **SEO:** Google Search Console + Bing Webmaster.

## 6. Legal notes (France / EU). Not legal advice; have counsel review before launch.

### 6.1 Cookies and consent (CNIL)
- ePrivacy (art. 82 of France's Loi Informatique et Libertés) requires consent before any read or write on the user's device unless it is strictly necessary. **Audience measurement is exempt** only when all these conditions hold: strictly limited to the site's own audience statistics, anonymous aggregate output, no cross-site tracking, no combination with other processing, identifiers limited to **13 months**, data kept **≤ 25 months**, and users informed with an opt-out.
- **Matomo** has an official CNIL configuration guide for the exemption. **Plausible** / **Umami** are cookieless, and practitioners consider them compatible, but they are not named in a CNIL list. Since Jan 2026 the CNIL uses a **self-assessment** tool instead of its former evaluation programme.
- **Google Analytics 4 is not exempt**, whatever its configuration. **PostHog**: product analytics and session replay need consent. Logged-in server-side events fall under GDPR (legitimate interest or contract, documented in the privacy policy and record of processing) rather than cookie consent.
- Other obligations: privacy policy in FR, DPA with each processor, EU hosting preferred (Vercel EU region, Supabase EU, PostHog EU), consent manager if any marketing pixels are used (use none at launch), and consumer law: 14-day withdrawal right, which the customer can waive for immediate digital access with explicit consent, and easy online cancellation (the French "résiliation en trois clics", in force since 1 June 2023).

### 6.3 The "HSK" name
- HSK / 汉语水平考试 is operated by **Chinese Testing International (CTI / 汉考国际)** under CLEC. Registered trademarks in China and elsewhere are **likely**, but ownership in the EU (EUIPO) and France (INPI) was **not verifiable** here. **[uncertain: search EUIPO eSearch and INPI before choosing a domain or brand]**
- Descriptive (nominative) use of "HSK" to say what content prepares for, e.g. "préparation au HSK", "HSK 3 vocabulary", is standard practice and generally acceptable.
- Do **not** use "HSK" in the product or company name or logo, imitate CTI branding, or imply official status. Add a disclaimer: « Hanlu n'est pas affilié à Chinese Testing International (CTI) ni au CLEC. HSK est une marque de ses titulaires respectifs. »
- Do not reproduce official past papers or mock tests, which are copyrighted. Write original mock items in the published format.
